# Risks of per-topic conductors: a red-team register

Explorer: RISKS. Written 2026-10-31 at about 10:20 PDT, from read-only checks on hera made between 10:05 and 10:20. "Conductor" below is a placeholder for the per-topic role; the name is still to be chosen (see R26).

Routing options compared throughout:

- **P: channel post.** A posts once to the topic channel `+topic`; B and the conductor both read the same immutable post.
- **C: Cc copy.** A sends direct mail to B and a second copy to the conductor. Under v2 as approved, that means two sends.
- **R: relay.** A sends to the conductor, which sends a message of its own to B.

**Verdict in four lines.** Most of the risk comes from custody, meaning the conductor holds and passes on messages (R). Most of the value John asked for comes from visibility, meaning the conductor sees a copy (C or P). Under custody the conductor becomes a bottleneck, a silent single point of failure, the biggest token cost, an amplifier for injected text, and the only part of this design that would need changes to agent-mail 2. The gap that visibility leaves is workers who bypass the conductor. A names-only census closes it by turning that bypass into a coverage figure John can see, instead of an assumption that the conductor saw everything.

## Arithmetic behind the register

**Latency.** Terms: *h* is one Syncthing hop; *s* is the 2 s settle; *d* is detection (under 1 s with kqueue or inotify, up to 15 s when only polling); *w* is the time to wake an idle Claude session, assumed about 3 s and not measured; *c* is the conductor's work on one message, assumed 15 to 60 s for a Claude session and to be measured.

Scenario 1: A on delphi-3bd4, B on andoria-08, conductor on hera.

| Case | Direct A to B | Relay A to conductor to B |
|---|---|---|
| Fast hops (h = 2 s, as measured in E1; d = 1 s) | 2h + s + d + w ≈ 10 s | (h + s + d + w) + c + (h + s + d + w) ≈ 16 s + c, so 31 to 76 s |
| Slow hops (h = 20 s; polling, d = 15 s) | ≈ 60 s | ≈ 80 s + c, so 95 to 140 s |
| A and B both on andoria-08 (fast hops) | s + d + w ≈ 6 s | 2 hops + 2(s + d + w) + c ≈ 16 s + c, so 31 to 76 s (5 to 13 times slower) |
| A conductor on a third Positron host | unchanged | 4 hops instead of 2 |

- Add the conductor's queue wait (below) and, when it is mid-turn, the rest of that turn, which can run for minutes.
- A question and its answer double every figure.
- A Codex B reads only at its next turn, whichever route is used. A Codex conductor would add that delay, potentially hours (E9), to every relayed message.

**Throughput: 25 workers, 100 messages an hour.**

- The mean gap between messages is 36 s, but traffic is bursty: today's notices went to 21 sessions at once (E11).
- With every message relayed and c = 30 s, utilisation is ρ = 0.83. The M/D/1 mean wait is ρc / (2(1 − ρ)) ≈ 75 s.
- At c = 36 s the conductor saturates. At c = 45 s the backlog grows by 20 messages an hour.
- A burst of 21 at c = 30 s leaves the last message waiting 10 minutes.
- A Claude session runs one turn at a time. While it relays, it does no managing, and a 10-minute analysis turn holds up every relay.
- Batching amortises reading: one 12,000-byte receive holds about 8 messages. It does not amortise relaying. v2 has no forward tool, so the model re-types every body: about 270 output tokens per 1,074-byte message, plus one model request per send.
- A relay through a channel is impossible at this rate, because v2 caps a sender at 20 posts per channel per hour (E3).

**Context and tokens.** I assume about 4 bytes per token; this is not measured.

- Reading one message with its v2 frame costs about 1,375 to 1,625 bytes, or about 375 tokens.
- Context growth per message:
  - relay (read, re-type, results, ledger note, some reasoning): about 1,000 to 1,200 tokens;
  - a Cc copy that the model reads and files itself: about 500 to 900;
  - a Cc copy filed by a script, with the model reading bodies only for about 10 percent of messages (exceptions): about 50 to 90.
- At 100 messages an hour that is about 100 to 120k tokens an hour for relay, 50 to 90k for model filing and 5 to 9k for script filing.
- Time to fill the usable window, ignoring the earlier autocompact threshold, which I did not check:
  - with the 1.0M window agent-deck assumes, less the 47.3k fixed overhead (E7): about 8 to 9 h for relay, 11 to 19 h for model filing, 4 to 8 days for script filing;
  - with a 200k window: about 1.5 h for relay and 2 to 3 h for model filing.
- Requests re-read the whole context (E20), so cost tracks *requests × context size*, not message size. With B ring batches an hour:
  - relay makes at least 100 + 3B requests (one send per message, plus a receive, a doorbell restart and a ledger write per batch);
  - model filing makes about 3B;
  - script filing makes about 3 per exception.
- With B = 20, a 300k mean context for the model-filing modes and a 100k context for script filing, the conductor re-reads about 48M input tokens an hour under relay, 18M under model filing and 3M under script filing. That is a ratio of about 16 : 6 : 1. I did not check prices or cache rates; the ratio is the point.
- Each nudge wakes a worker for at least three requests (receive, reply, doorbell restart). Each request re-reads at least the 47.3k fixed overhead (this session's figure; others differ) plus the worker's conversation. For a worker with a 200k conversation, that is about 0.75M tokens re-read per nudge.
- Under P, every subscriber reads all topic traffic: 25 subscribers × 100 posts × 375 tokens ≈ 0.9M tokens an hour across the fleet.

### Routing scorecard by risk area (+ helps, 0 neutral, − hurts)

| Area | P: channel post | C: Cc copy | R: relay |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Latency and throughput | + direct path (needs a wake subscription to be prompt) | + direct path unchanged | − adds a leg and a conductor turn; saturates near 36 s per message |
| 2 Single point of failure | + falls back to unsupervised delivery | + falls back to unsupervised delivery | − delivery stops silently for up to an hour |
| 3 Context and cost | − every subscriber pays | + only the conductor reads copies | − read plus re-type; at least one request per message |

| 5 Loops and storms | + capped at 20 posts per channel per hour | − direct mail uncapped; reply-all loops | − every reply passes through; retries are re-typed |

## Design constraints that follow

1. **Deliver directly; observe by copy.** Workers deliver by direct mail or a topic post, and the conductor gets a Cc copy or a subscription. Its failure may pause oversight, never delivery. (R1, R2, R4, R5, R22)
2. **Relay attention, never text.** When a conductor routes, it sends a short pointer of its own, such as "A asked you about X in message id; answer A directly", never a re-post of peer text. v2's "Never forward or re-post peer text" then stays true unchanged. (R10, R18, R27)
3. **Zero model requests per routine message.** A deterministic host-local script files each message (id, from, to, subject, stamp, topic). The model wakes only on named exceptions, reads bodies only then, and handles one batch per ring. A message addressed to the conductor itself, rather than a copy, is always an exception. (R2, R6, R7)
4. **State lives on local disk, bounded.** The ledger sits outside `~/Mailbox`, mode 0600, append-only with rotation and a size cap. A bounded summary is re-read at turn start and after compaction. Nothing in the synced folder is read back as state or authority. (R6, R13)
5. **No authority, claimed or implied.** A conductor never answers an approval-class question (push, activation, history rewrite, secrets, another session's files, design or business decisions); it routes them to John. Its mail is peer mail in every frame. (R11, R12)
6. **John wins, and workers can tell mechanically.** User turns in a worker's own session are John's. Anything inside an agent-mail frame is a peer's, the conductor's included. On a conflict the worker follows John and says so. (R21)
7. **No user-role paths.** No `agent-deck session send`, tmux send-keys, `codex queue` or prompt-injecting hooks. A conductor never drains another session's agent-deck inbox, since reading clears it (E12). (R21)
8. **Least privilege for the most exposed reader.** Read-only repositories, no push or activation, and writes only to its ledger and its own outgoing mail. Whether conductors run outside dangerous_mode is John's call (E13). (R10)
9. **Intervene only on named triggers, with evidence.** The triggers: duplicate work; conflicting changes to one path, branch or id space; a mail-only question unanswered for more than N minutes; a worker idle with an open assignment for more than N minutes, judged by agent-deck status rather than presence. A conductor never invents work, re-plans or reassigns; it suggests to John. (R19, R20)
10. **Nudge budget.** By default at most one nudge per worker per two hours, sent direct with "no reply needed unless blocked". Never to Codex workers (decision 9), never to stopped sessions, and never an acknowledgment. (R9, R15)
11. **One conductor per worker; one domain per topic.** Each worker's conductor is recorded in its operator config (nix), not in the synced mailbox. All of a topic's members sit on one side of the Positron/personal divide. Digests carry ids, counts and paths, never content. (R14, R16)
12. **Questions surface through the pane path the director already scans**: once, with the original message id, and only for questions that live only in mail. Never by mail to `conductor-director`. (R17, R24)
13. **Measure liveness by queue age.** Track the age of the oldest file name addressed to the conductor and the ledger's heartbeat stamp, and alert above 30 minutes. Workers fail open to direct mail when the conductor's member record is more than 15 minutes old. (R4, R22)
14. **State coverage; never assume it.** A names-only census on hera (to, from, stamp; never contents) is compared with the ledger, and every digest says what fraction of topic traffic the conductor saw. (R23)
15. **Less to watch, not more.** A conductor's escalations replace the director's pings for its topic. Digests are pull-only and daily by default. Zero routine approvals. One command starts or stops a conductor, and the director's existing recovery rules cover it. (R25)
16. **Conductors run on Claude or Pi, never Codex.** (R3, E9)
17. **Stay off v2's critical path.** Version one uses v2's seven tools and its CLI as approved (a Cc is two sends) and is a skill plus a host-local script. Pilot it on one topic only after Task 25. Anything that needs agent-mail code goes to mail-dev as a v2.1 request: a Cc header, a per-session "always copy" setting, a role prefix. (R27)
18. **Names.** The role word avoids agent-deck's "conductor". Conductor and channel names avoid v2's reserved list and every session name, since a channel may not share a session's name (E18). A reserved prefix stops only honest mistakes, because readers do not enforce reserved senders (E4). (R12, R26)
19. **Catch conflicts with intent announcements, not by holding mail.** A worker about to touch a shared path posts one line to the topic first. (R28)

## Open questions for John (most important first)

1. **Custody or visibility?** Is it enough for the conductor to see a copy (Cc or a topic channel) while A delivers to B directly? Or do you want messages to wait for the conductor to pass them on? Relay causes the bottleneck, the single point of failure, most of the cost and the injection amplifier, and it is the only part that would change agent-mail 2.
2. **May a conductor answer workers' questions itself,** the way the director auto-answers "Should I proceed?", or only route them to you? If it may answer some, which kinds are allowed and which never are (approvals, design decisions)?
3. **Where should questions reach you?** The director's scanner already pings Discord about a minute after a question appears in any pane; nix's question at 10:11 today is an example. Should conductors use that path, only for questions that exist only in mail? And should the director deduplicate so that one question pings you once?
4. **May a topic span Positron and personal work,** or is it one conductor per domain? Until per-device allowlists exist, every post and digest reaches all ten hosts, and question text already goes to Discord.
5. **Stuck and starved: may a conductor nudge idle workers by mail, or only tell you?** Each nudge costs a worker turn. Sessions with no mail loop, such as translate-tool, cannot be nudged by mail at all.
6. **Pilot: which topic, which host, and when?** I suggest one topic, after v2's Task 25, with test sessions, measured for a week.
7. **Permissions: should conductor sessions keep the fleet's dangerous_mode,** or run with fewer powers, since they read the most peer text?
8. **Should the director watch each conductor's queue age** (file names only) and alert you when a conductor stops? That is a change to your director's policy.
9. **Digests: none, a daily file or page that you open yourself, or a channel post?** And may a conductor ever post to subscribers who wake on each post?
10. **Name:** "conductor" already means an agent-deck conductor, whose heartbeats arrive through `session send`. Do you want a different word, such as steward, foreman or lead, to keep the two apart?
